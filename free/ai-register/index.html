AI register

List your AI tools and agents, who owns each, the data it touches and its risk, in the Australian Government's free AI register template. We fill in what each vendor documents.

Your register stays in your browser. Nothing you enter leaves it. Typed text, such as purpose notes, never goes into your saved profile or account.

Saving is not available right now, so the register lasts until you close or reload. Download the CSV before you leave.

    The columns

    The twelve columns of the National AI Centre's free template (Guidance for AI Adoption), in its order, with its status and screening choices. Picklists keep what you type out of your saved profile.

    Template columnHow we fill it
    Name and versionFrom our knowledge base, or your own name. Vendors rarely pin a model version for hosted tools, so we do not guess one.
    OwnerA role, not a person's name.
    StatusPilot, Operational or Decommissioned, as in the template.
    SourceThe vendor. For agents, who built it and the platform it runs on.
    Purpose/business goalsYour own words, kept on this device only.
    Intended use case(s)Picked from common uses.
    Known limitations and prohibited usePicked from common rules, such as no personal information.
    Any foreseeable misusePicked from common misuse, such as personal accounts.
    Data sources and typeBanded categories and types.
    Registered dateThe day you add the row, editable.
    Screening outcomeNormal, Elevated or Unacceptable, as in the template. We suggest Normal or Elevated. The government's AI screening tool gives the formal result.
    Key stakeholders affectedPicked from a list.

    We add users, hosting route and region, account type, review date and vendor facts for six key controls. Agent rows add the identity they act under, the tools they can call and their human oversight. The AICD and UTS AI governance checklist for SME and NFP directors lists an AI register first for boards.
