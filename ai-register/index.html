List the AI tools and agents your business uses, who owns each one, what data it touches and how risky it is. The columns follow the Australian Government's free AI register template, and we fill in what each vendor documents from our knowledge base.

Your register stays in your browser. Nothing you enter leaves it. Purpose notes and other typed text never go into your saved profile or your account. They stay on this device.

    The columns

    The Guidance for AI Adoption from the National AI Centre includes a free AI register template. We use its twelve columns, in its order, and its own choices for status and screening outcome. Where we can, we offer picklists instead of free text, so your register can be saved with your profile without storing what you typed.

    Template columnHow we fill it
    Name and versionThe platform and hosting route from our knowledge base, or the name you give. Vendors rarely pin a model version for hosted tools, so we do not guess one.
    OwnerA role, not a person's name. Add the person in your own copy.
    StatusPilot, Operational or Decommissioned, as in the template.
    SourceThe vendor from our knowledge base. For agents, who built it and which platform it runs on.
    Purpose/business goalsYour own words, kept on this device only.
    Intended use case(s)Picked from a list of common uses.
    Known limitations and prohibited usePicked from common rules, such as no personal information or a person checks every output.
    Any foreseeable misusePicked from common misuse, such as staff using a personal account.
    Data sources and typeBanded data categories and data types.
    Registered dateThe day you add the row. You can change it.
    Screening outcomeNormal, Elevated or Unacceptable, as in the template. We suggest Normal or Elevated from your answers. The government's AI screening tool gives the formal result.
    Key stakeholders affectedPicked from a list.

    We add columns the template leaves to you: who uses each tool, the hosting route and region, the account type, a review date, and what the vendor documents for six controls a register reader asks about first. Agents get their own rows, with the identity they act under, the tools they can call and the human oversight in place. The AICD and UTS AI governance checklist for SME and NFP directors puts an AI register first in its list for boards.

    What to do next

    Give each row an owner, set a review date, and check every Elevated row before you widen its use. If you want help with the Elevated rows, the vendor gaps or a full AI governance setup, write to us.
